# Study gait features in the results table

The overall and side-specific tables together include all 25 reference-supported study features. The side-specific table also includes additional step, velocity, stance and swing outputs whose individual side-specific reference support has not been established. The CSV contains both tables; the JSON retains the full analysis. Unavailable values are shown explicitly. Existing walking-range selection, full-person visibility, turn detection, and manual segment editing are unchanged. Turn values represent the dominant selected turn (largest absolute yaw change); if no turn is selected or detected they are unavailable. Adjusting boundaries recomputes the same rows from cached poses and events.

Stride time and length use consecutive same-foot contacts within each straight segment. Following the archived study extension, stride displacement uses the segment's horizontal pelvis progression direction and reconstructed ankle coordinates without the extra height calibration multiplier used by routine step length. Study stride sample filtering and pooled sample means are retained. This makes stride and step length scaling different; use care when interpreting or comparing them.

Step-width asymmetry is the per-segment normalized side difference, 200 * abs(L-R)/(L+R), weighted across segments by the smaller side count, requiring at least two width samples per side. Arm amplitude asymmetry is the study symmetry-angle index weighted by the number of steps. Both are calculated per inference pass and then averaged across original and mirrored passes, like the other outputs.

The output definitions, camera calibration, selected intervals and event boundary handling can differ from the historical validation analyses; inclusion here does not establish identical validation of this implementation. In the study, arm amplitude asymmetry, step-width asymmetry, arm correlation, trunk ROM and both turn measures fell below the repeatability screen. Missing or inadequate data should not be interpreted as zero.
